Exam pattern and qualifying marks

Preliminary examination

Objective OMR paper, two hours, with no negative marking for wrong answers. Prelims marks are used for screening.

The 2025 notice sets a 45% minimum for most candidates and 40% for SC/ST, persons with benchmark disabilities and ex-servicemen, with a further relaxation provision for ex-servicemen.

Main written examination

Two descriptive law papers and separate Hindi and English essays. The standard interview threshold is 35% in each law paper and 40% overall.

In the 2025 notice, SC/ST, persons with benchmark disabilities and ex-servicemen have minimums of 30% in each law paper and 35% overall.

Interview

The 35-mark interview assesses suitability for judicial service. The rules also provide for assessment of knowledge of Rajasthani dialects and local social customs.

Final merit combines mains and interview marks.

The qualifying minimum is a floor. The actual shortlist cutoff depends on category-wise vacancies and candidate scores.

Eligibility

These requirements are drawn from the High Court’s 2025 recruitment notice. Check the notice for your recruitment cycle before applying.

Qualification

Indian citizenship and a recognised LL.B. (Professional). The 2025 notice allowed candidates appearing in the final year or semester to apply if they qualified before mains and submitted proof within the prescribed window.

Age

For the 2025 recruitment, candidates had to be at least 21 and under 40 on 1 January 2026, before applicable relaxations. Consult the notice for category-specific relaxations and conditions.

Language and local knowledge

Candidates must have thorough knowledge of Hindi written in Devanagari, Rajasthani dialects and the social customs of Rajasthan.

Prelims and mains syllabus

Prelims law questions draw from the subjects prescribed for both mains law papers. The Hindi and English component tests grammar, vocabulary and usage.

Law Paper I · Civil

Civil procedure, constitutional law, contract, evidence, limitation, specific relief, transfer of property, statutory interpretation, Hindu and Muslim law, partnership, sale of goods, registration, and order and judgment writing. Rajasthan topics include rent control, court fees and suits valuation, and land revenue.

Law Paper II · Criminal

Criminal procedure, penal law and evidence, plus juvenile justice, negotiable instruments, probation, domestic violence, indecent representation, POCSO, workplace sexual harassment, Rajasthan excise, information technology, framing charges and judgment writing.

Hindi proficiency

Practise sandhi and samas, word choice, vocabulary, sentence correction, idioms, punctuation, and administrative and legal terminology.

English proficiency

Practise tenses, articles and determiners, idioms and phrasal verbs, voice, conjunctions, reported speech, modals, synonyms and antonyms.

What to prioritise

Prepare both languages

Hindi and English together carry 30% of prelims. Practise Hindi separately from the PYQ bank and write timed essays in both languages for mains.

Revise procedure and evidence

In the supplied sample, 16 questions are tagged Criminal Procedure, 11 Evidence and 9 IPC. Use these counts to find practice material and the official syllabus to plan coverage.

Build a Rajasthan statutes checklist

Revise the forums, powers, deadlines and remedies under the Rajasthan Rent Control, Court Fees and Suits Valuation, Land Revenue and Excise Acts. Practise civil orders and criminal charges for mains.

Frequently asked questions

Does RJS prelims have a separate general studies paper?

The High Court’s 2025 scheme specifies one objective paper covering law and Hindi and English proficiency.

Do prelims marks count toward final selection?

Prelims scores determine eligibility for the mains shortlist. Final merit uses mains and interview marks.

How many marks does each stage carry?

Prelims carries 100 marks, mains carries 300 marks across four written papers, and the interview carries 35 marks under the 2025 scheme.

Where can I check the rules and recruitment notices?

See the 2025 detailed advertisement and syllabus, Rajasthan Judicial Service Rules, Schedule IV, and the High Court recruitment page.
